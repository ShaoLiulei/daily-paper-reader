Abstract 

This work presents the Parallelized Hierarchical Connectome (PHC), a gen-eral architectural framework that upgrades temporal-only State-Space Mod-els (SSMs) into spatiotemporal recurrent networks. Conventional SSMs achieve high-speed sequence processing through parallel scans, yet they are funda-mentally limited to temporal recurrence and lack the capacity for lateral or feedback interactions within a single timestep. PHC addresses this by structurally mapping the diagonal SSM core to a shared Neuron Layer and inter-neuronal communication to a shared Synapse Layer, where neurons are partitioned into hierarchical regions and connectivity is governed by the connectome topology. A Multi-Transmission Loop enables intra-slice spatial recurrence, allowing signals to propagate recurrently across the hierarchical connectome within each temporal window while preserving O(logT) temporal parallelism. This spatiotemporal framework enables the seamless integration of neuro-physical priors typically intractable for standard SSMs, including adaptive leaky integrate-and-fire dynamics, Dale’s Law, short-term plastic-ity, and reward-modulated spike-timing-dependent plasticity. The framework is instantiated as PHCSSM, the first model to unify recurrent spiking neural network dynamics with diagonal SSM parallelism while simultaneously en-

1. Introduction 

The advent of linear State-Space Models (SSMs), such as S4 and Mamba, has advanced sequence modeling by bridging the gap between the expressive power of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and the parallelizable training efficiency of Transformers (Gu et al., 2022a; Hasani et al., 2022; Smith et al., 2023; Orvieto et al., 2023; Rusch and Rus, 2025). By formulating sequence processing through a linear time-invariant (LTI) lens, these models leverage associative parallel scans to achieve O(log L) training complexity, effectively overcoming the vanishing gradient and sequential dependency bottlenecks inherent in traditional Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) (Gu et al., 2022a). However, this computational leap has come at the cost of structural biological realism. To maintain parallel efficiency, modern SSMs mathemat-ically constrain their state transition matrices to be diagonal (Gupta et al., 2022; Gu et al., 2022b; Orvieto et al., 2023; Gonzalez et al., 2024; Farsang et al., 2025). While this diagonal Jacobian approximation enables scalability, it obfuscates the physical reality of neural networks by compressing intrinsic cellular decay and inter-neuronal communication into a single operator (Gu et al., 2022a,b; Farsang et al., 2025). Consequently, neurons at the same timestep remain mutually decoupled, precluding the complex spatial feed-back, lateral inhibition, and hierarchical interactions observed in biological cortical circuits. While biologically-inspired Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) and RNNs possess these rich spatiotemporal dynamics (Yu et al., 2022; Bellec et al., 2020; Lei et al., 2018), their inherent non-linearity and dense recursive dependencies force a strictly sequential execution. This creates a prohibitive 2training bottleneck where each timestep must wait for the completion of all synaptic transmissions from the previous state, rendering them incompatible with modern parallel hardware for long-duration sequences. Historically, modeling long-range dependencies via recurrent networks has been computationally bottlenecked by BPTT, where the network’s logical depth is inherently entangled with the sequence length (Bengio et al., 1994; Pascanu et al., 2013). While modern SSMs resolve temporal dependencies via parallel associative scans, they fail to decouple this depth. To compensate for the lack of intra-step spatial expressivity, current state-of-the-art frame-works resort to "layer stacking", the deep concatenation of L independent SSM blocks interleaved with Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) (Figure 1A) (Gonzalez et al., 2024; Farsang et al., 2025). While effective at increasing model capacity, this strategy incurs a significant linear growth in param-eter complexity, Θ( D2L), creating a massive memory footprint unsuitable for resource-constrained deployment. More importantly, it deviates from the fundamental principles of the brain, which achieves immense functional depth by re-routing signals through a fixed, recurrent physical connectome (Khalil et al., 2017; Schmutz et al., 2020) rather than expanding through a linear sequence of distinct, non-communicating layers. In contrast to the prevailing trend of scaling up dense, structureless ma-trices to capture sequence dependencies, it is argued here that the phys-ical constraints governing biological microcircuits offer powerful inductive biases for parameter-efficient and robust sequence modeling. Traditionally, strict neuro-physical priors, such as Dale’s Law (the segregation of excitatory and inhibitory neurons), macro-scale regional connectomes, and Short-Term Plasticity (STP), were viewed as computational burdens that might hamper the gradient-based optimization of artificial models (Cornford et al., 2021; Balwani et al., 2025). However, these constraints actually function as sta-bilizing mechanisms that regulate internal dynamics and information flow (Haber and Schneidman, 2022; Schmutz et al., 2020). For instance, the in-corporation of Adaptive Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (ALIF) dynamics allows a network to maintain "memory" of past activations through intrinsic cellular mechanisms, rather than relying solely on dense external recurrent weights (Teeter et al., 2018; Bellec et al., 2018). Similarly, STP transforms static structural connections into time-varying, state-dependent effective weights, enabling the network to adapt to temporal statistics on the fly (Mongillo et al., 2008; Tsodyks and Markram, 1997). By hardwiring these biological constraints directly into the network architecture, a model can substitute spatial parameter scale with temporal dynamic expressivity, ef-fectively demonstrating that competitive sequence modeling capabilities can be maintained under strict biological constraints using only a fraction of the parameters required by generic deep networks. This architectural landscape reveals a fundamental trade-off in modern sequence modeling: learnable lateral spatial connections (inter-neuron state-to-state weights within recurrence) and temporal parallel scan efficiency have been mutually exclusive. Models that preserve O(L) or O(log L) parallel scan (including S4, S4D, S5, Mamba, LRU, and the recent PD-SSM (Terzić et al., 2025)) universally restrict the state transition matrix to diagonal (or permutation-diagonal) form, precluding weighted inter-dimension interac-tion. Conversely, models that support lateral connections (such as classical RNNs and the sLSTM component of xLSTM (Beck et al., 2024)) sacrifice parallelizability entirely. To reconcile this fundamental dichotomy, the Paral-lelized Hierarchical Connectome (PHC) framework is proposed, which is, to 4the best of the author’s knowledge, the first SSM framework that introduces learnable lateral connection weights within the SSM recurrence structure while preserving temporal parallel scan efficiency. PHC fundamentally re-constructs the recurrent formulation through Intra-Step Spatiotemporal De-coupling (Figure 1B), identifying a precise structural isomorphism between modern SSM building blocks and neural operators: the diagonal SSM core maps to PHC’s spike module in neuron layer (NL) and presynapse mod-ule in synapse layer (SL), while the inter-layer mixing matrix maps to the postsynapse module in SL. Unlike conventional stacked architectures that replicate independent parameters across depth (Gu et al., 2022b; Gonzalez et al., 2024; Farsang et al., 2025), PHC collapses this depth into a single spa-tial dimension by partitioning the neurons of a shared NL into R hierarchical regions. Communication is then routed through a shared, sparsely-masked SL. To overcome the mutual decoupling imposed by diagonal Jacobian con-straints, PHC introduces the Multi-transmission Loop (Figure 2). Within a single timestep, sensory signals and hidden states recurrently circulate across the separated NL and SL. Rather than executing a static feedforward pass, this recursive routing allows the non-linear network to dynamically settle into a sequence-level fixed point (a stable dynamic attractor) prior to tem-poral progression. This intra-slice spatial recurrence effectively substitutes the depth of L independent layers with an M-step spatial circulation, achiev-ing deep logical processing across a physical connectome with only Θ( D2)

parameters. This parallelizability rests on a key observation: from dendritic integration through spike generation to synaptic release, each neuron’s pro-cessing pipeline is entirely cell-autonomous and does not depend on any other neuron’s concurrent state. Cross-neuron interaction occurs only at the trans-mission boundary, where the SL’s matrix multiplication fans out spikes and aggregates inputs, an operation that is independent across timesteps. The M transmission steps therefore track spatial interaction depth rather than temporal history, yielding a total computational depth of O(M log T) with the two axes fully orthogonal (see Section 5 for a detailed analysis). The PHC framework is built on a single architectural principle: Intra-Step Spatiotemporal Decoupling, separating temporal and spatial dynamics into distinct components while coupling them bidirectionally within each recur-rence step via the Multi-Transmission Loop. This principle can upgrade any temporal-only diagonal SSM into a spatiotemporal recurrent network by de-coupling the temporal parallel scan from the spatial hierarchical connectome and connecting them via the Multi-Transmission Loop. Empirical evaluation on six physiological benchmarks from the UEA Mul-tivariate Time-Series Classification Archive (Walker et al., 2024; Rusch and 7Rus, 2025; Farsang et al., 2025) demonstrates that PHCSSM maintains highly competitive sequence modeling capabilities even under these severe neuro-physical constraints. Notably, PHCSSM achieves 59.3% on SCP2 (the high-est reported SSM accuracy on this benchmark, surpassing LinOSS-IMEX (58.9%)) and outperforms Mamba on the long-horizon MotorImagery task by 6.0 percentage points (53.7%). These results establish that biologically grounded spatiotemporal recurrence and structured sparsity serve as highly effective, stabilizing inductive biases for deep sequence modeling, rather than performance bottlenecks. In summary, the main contributions are as follows:. 

• Intra-Step Spatiotemporal Decoupling: The First Parallelizable SSM with Lateral Connections: The PHC framework is proposed to resolve the fundamental trade-off between learnable lateral connections and parallel scan efficiency by decoupling the recurrence into a Tempo-ral Parallel Scan (diagonal, O(log L) parallelizable) and a Spatial Hi-erarchical Connectome (off-diagonal, weighted many-to-many lateral connections), connected via a Multi-Transmission Loop with Cauchy convergence. Unlike PD-SSM’s permutation routing (one-to-one copy without learnable weights) and xLSTM’s sLSTM (lateral connections but not parallelizable), PHC uniquely provides weighted spatiotempo-ral recurrence within the SSM framework while preserving O(log L)training efficiency. 

• Parameter Efficiency: By substituting sequential layer-stacking with the Multi-transmission Loop over a shared hierarchical connectome, PHCSSM achieves a 1/L reduction in parameter count relative to stacked architectures, maintaining a complexity of Θ( D2).

• Parallelized Neuro-physical Dynamics: Mathematical formulations are derived that translate saturating STP and adaptive membrane dynam-ics into log-domain parallel scans, enabling dynamic synaptic efficacy without sacrificing scalability. 

• Native Online Learning: A reward-modulated STDP module is intro-duced that utilizes genuine binary spikes to provide locally causal learn-ing signals, complementing gradient-based optimization. 2.1. State-Space Models and the Diagonal Jacobian Constraint 

Modern sequence modeling has been transformed by structured State-Space Models (SSMs) that recast temporal dependencies as linear dynamical systems solvable via parallel associative scans in O(log L) sequential depth. The architectural lineage from S4 (Gu et al., 2022a) through S4D (Gu et al., 2022b), S5 (Smith et al., 2023), and LRU (Orvieto et al., 2023) progressively demonstrated that restricting state-transition matrices to diagonal form pre-serves modeling capacity while enabling efficient parallelization. A critical limitation of these time-invariant models is their inability to adaptively filter input content. Mamba (Gu and Dao, 2023) addresses this by making its state-transition parameters input-dependent, enabling selective content filtering, while S7 (Soydan et al., 2024) applies input dependence to the purely recur-rent S5 and introduces a stable reparameterization of the transition matrix with formal guarantees on forward state boundedness and gradient stabil-ity, a property absent in Mamba and Liquid-S4. LinOSS (Rusch and Rus, 2025) departs from the purely diagonal paradigm by employing second-order harmonic oscillator dynamics with block-diagonal state transitions ( 2 × 2

blocks per oscillatory mode), capturing richer temporal features while still precluding cross-neuron interaction within a timestep. For genuinely non-linear recurrences, the DEER framework (Lim et al., 2024) formulates state updates as fixed-point problems solvable via Newton’s method with parallel scans at O(T D 3) cost; ELK (Gonzalez et al., 2024) reduces this to O(TD) via diagonal Jacobian approximations. LrcSSM (Farsang et al., 2025) takes a more principled route by redesigning the underlying liquid-resistance, liquid-capacitance neural network so that its Jacobian is inherently diagonal, retain-ing only self-loop synaptic parameters, yielding exact parallel updates with state-and-input-dependent dynamics, and empirical ablations confirm no per-formance loss from this structural simplification. Across this entire family, however, no model introduces within-timestep spatial interaction among neu-rons: representational depth is universally recovered through stacking inde-pendent blocks, each maintaining its own parameter set. This structural limitation is revisited in Section 2.4, where recent attempts are surveyed to introduce lateral connections or spatial structure into SSMs (including PD-SSM, xLSTM, GraphS4mer, and GraphMamba) and identify the gap that the PHC framework fills. 92.2. Spiking State-Space Models 

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) offer energy-efficient computation through binary spike communication, but the sequential dependence of membrane potential on spiking history creates a fundamental barrier to parallel train-ing on long sequences. Binary-S4D (Stan and Rhodes, 2024) applies binary activation directly to S4D hidden states, enabling parallel training but en-tirely neglecting neuronal dynamics such as reset and refractory mechanisms. S6-based SNN (Bal and Sengupta, 2024) introduces stochastic spiking neu-rons to enhance sparsity at the cost of accuracy. SpikingSSM (Shen et al., 2025) pre-trains a Surrogate Dynamic Network to approximate LIF dynam-ics, accelerating inference but constraining applicability to neuron models amenable to such approximation. SPikE-SSM (Zhong et al., 2024) offers a more principled solution by decomposing membrane potential into parallel-computable components, achieving substantial training speedup on long se-quences. Nonetheless, all existing Spiking SSMs treat inter-neuronal con-nections as unconstrained dense matrices without Dale’s Law enforcement, employ static synaptic weights without Short-Term Plasticity, and rely ex-clusively on surrogate gradients for weight updates without any spike-timing-based online learning rule. 

2.3. Biologically Grounded Dynamics in Recurrent Models 

A growing body of work incorporates neuroscience-inspired priors into recurrent sequence models. At the single-neuron level, the liquid neural net-work family, from LTCs (Hasani et al., 2021) with state-and-input-dependent time constants, through STCs (Farsang et al., 2024a) with sigmoidal gat-ing, to LRCs (Farsang et al., 2024b) incorporating state-and-input-dependent membrane capacitance, progressively integrates richer biophysical phenom-ena into trainable dynamics. LrcSSM scales LRCs to long sequences and demonstrates that liquid capacitance consistently outperforms constant-capa-citance models; its discussion also highlights a complementarity between bi-ological inspirations, noting that LinOSS models cortical harmonic oscilla-tions while LRCs model chemical synapse transmission; these are distinct neurophysiological phenomena that both yield competitive performance. At the synaptic level, the Tsodyks–Markram formalism models STP through coupled facilitation and depression variables, but the non-linear coupling be-tween these variables creates sequential dependencies that have resisted inte-gration into parallel training pipelines. At the network level, Dale’s Law and 10 STDP provide fundamental structural and learning priors explored in clas-sical SNN literature but rarely in conjunction with parallelizable SSMs. To date, these biophysical mechanisms (ALIF adaptive spiking dynamics, STP, Dale’s Law, topology-constrained hierarchical connectivity, and R-STDP) have each been studied in isolation; no existing framework unifies them within a single architecture that supports O(log L) parallel training. 

2.4. Lateral Connections and Spatial Interaction in Sequence Models 

A central architectural question for SSMs is whether inter-dimension (lat-eral) interaction can be introduced within the recurrence without forfeiting parallel scan efficiency. The most relevant attempts are surveyed below, de-lineating how each differs from the PHC framework proposed in this work. PD-SSM. The Permutation-and-Diagonal SSM (Terzić et al., 2025) fac-torizes the state transition matrix as A(u) = P (u) · D(u), preserving O(LN )

parallel scan complexity via PD monoid closure. However, the permutation 

P performs one-to-one position routing rather than weighted many-to-many aggregation, and all learnable parameters reside on the input-to-parameter pathway; PD-SSM contains no learnable state-to-state lateral connection weights. xLSTM. Extended Long Short-Term Memory (Beck et al., 2024) intro-duces two complementary cell designs: sLSTM, with genuine learnable lateral connections via recurrence matrices R, and mLSTM, which achieves paral-lelizability via an outer-product matrix memory. Critically, sLSTM’s lateral connections render it non-parallelizable, while mLSTM sacrifices lateral con-nections entirely; xLSTM thus does not resolve the trade-off. GraphS4mer. For multivariate biosignal modeling, GraphS4mer (Tang et al., 2023) constructs a serial pipeline: S4 (temporal) → Graph Structure Learning → GNN (spatial), confining spatial interaction to a post-hoc ag-gregation step entirely outside the SSM recurrence. Similarly, GraphMamba (Behrouz and Hashemi, 2024) encodes graph structure in input preprocess-ing, leaving the diagonal Mamba backbone devoid of lateral connections. Taken together, these prior works share a common limitation: spatial and temporal processing either occur in sequential stages external to the recur-rence (GraphS4mer, GraphMamba), or are resolved by forfeiting one of the two properties (xLSTM). PD-SSM approximates spatial interaction within a single recurrence step, but restricts routing to a one-to-one permutation without iterated weighted aggregation. No existing model achieves within-timestep iterated lateral interaction within a parallelizable recurrence. This 11 is the gap that PHCSSM fills through Intra-Step Spatiotemporal Decoupling and the Multi-Transmission Loop. 

3. Methodology 

3.1. Architecture Overview 

PHCSSM is built on a single architectural principle: Intra-Step Spa-tiotemporal Decoupling. Conventional SSMs and their spiking variants ag-gregate intrinsic membrane dynamics and extrinsic synaptic interactions into a single dense state-transition operator. PHCSSM explicitly bifurcates this operator into two complementary structural components: a NL that encap-sulates the intrinsic temporal dynamics of individual neurons as a strictly diagonal recurrence, and a SL that mediates all inter-neuronal communica-tion through two functionally distinct sub-modules: the Pre-synapse Module (diagonal parallel scans for STP dynamics) and the Post-synapse Module (spatial lateral communication via the biologically constrained weight matrix 

Wstruct ) (Figure 3). This separation is not merely a notational convenience; it is the structural prerequisite that enables parallelized training and faithful biological grounding simultaneously. To aid intuition, Figure 2 provides an explicit operator-level mapping between a conventional L-layer stacked SSM and the PHC framework. In the stacked design, each of the L diagonal state-transition layers maintains an independent parameter set, and inter-layer communication is mediated by L separate dense MLPs with non-shared weights, forming a strictly uni-directional forward path. PHC collapses this vertical hierarchy into a sin-gle horizontal plane: the L independent diagonal layers become R regions within a shared Neuron Layer, and the L independent MLPs become sub-blocks of a single Hierarchical Connectome Matrix within a shared Synapse Layer. This consolidation has two consequences. First, parameter sharing across regions and transmission steps reduces the total parameter count from 

Θ( D2L) to Θ( D2). Second, the block-structured Connectome Matrix natu-rally accommodates bidirectional inter-region projections (feedforward and feedback) as well as intra-region lateral connections, connectivity patterns that are structurally impossible in the unidirectional stacked pipeline. The Multi-Transmission Loop then recovers the logical processing depth lost by collapsing layers: M iterative circulations between the shared NL and SL achieve depth-M spatial recurrence, functionally equivalent to an M-layer 12 stacked network but with shared weights and dynamic early exit via the Cauchy convergence criterion (Eq. 14). The raw input sequence x ∈ RB×T ×Din is first projected into the neuron dimension via a linear encoder with layer normalization, then gated by a binary input mask min ∈ { 0, 1}D that restricts sensory drive to designated input populations (e.g., excitatory neurons in region R0): 

xsensory = LayerNorm (Wenc x + benc ) ⊙ min (1) During training, the entire forward pass operates in the time domain. Because all NL dynamics (ALIF membrane decay, adaptive threshold) and SL dynamics (Short-Term Plasticity) have been reformulated as affine recur-rences, the full sequence of length T is processed via log-domain parallel pre-fix sums (logcumsumexp) in O(log T ) sequential depth, completely bypassing the O(T ) bottleneck of Backpropagation Through Time. During inference, the parallel scan is replaced by step-by-step spatial-domain propagation.

4.3. Computational Efficiency 

PHCSSM’s weight-sharing architecture differs fundamentally from con-ventional stacked designs (Figures 1 and 2). In standard SSMs, L copies of a diagonal state-space core and an MLP are arranged sequentially in depth, yielding Θ( D2L) total parameters (Table 6). The Multi-Transmission Loop reuses a single NL–SL pair across all M transmission steps, reducing parame-ter scaling to Θ( D2), achieving depth-M spatial recurrence at a fraction of the parameter cost. Concretely, PHCSSM’s parameter counts (1,748–9,485) are one to two orders of magnitude smaller than those of comparably performing SSMs: S5 requires 67,554–205,826 parameters, Mamba uses 66,818–400,642, and even the relatively compact LrcSSM employs 6,674–38,282 (Table 6). The intra-step spatiotemporal decoupling further contributes to train-ing efficiency. Because all biological dynamics, including ALIF membrane evolution, STP-mediated synaptic modulation, and spike generation, are re-formulated as affine recurrences solvable via log-domain parallel prefix sums, the temporal dimension is processed at O(log T) sequential depth rather than the O(T) cost of conventional BPTT. This logarithmic scaling is what makes training on ultra-long sequences such as EigenWorms (17,984 timesteps) tractable; a sequential spiking network of equivalent depth would face pro-hibitive wall-clock time under standard backpropagation. Discussion 

A central finding of this work is that strict biological constraints need not impose a performance penalty and may instead serve as beneficial induc-tive biases. The ablation analysis (Section 4.4) reveals that each biological constraint contributes a distinct and non-redundant accuracy improvement: removing Dale’s Law causes a mean accuracy drop of –3.70pp across the three ablated benchmarks, indicating that the E/I sign constraint serves as a struc-tural prior guiding optimization. The R-STDP mechanism is uniquely en-abled by PHCSSM’s genuine binary spike representation: unlike continuous-valued SSMs, the model can apply the biologically grounded STDP rule without surrogate approximation, providing a local Hebbian learning signal orthogonal to the global backpropagation gradient. Together, these results suggest that bio-constrained SSMs constitute an underexplored yet promising class of structured priors for sequence modeling. Beyond the spiking instantiation presented here, the PHC framework’s core principle, Intra-Step Spatiotemporal Decoupling, is applicable to any diagonal SSM. All existing parallelizable SSMs (S4D, S5, LRU, Mamba, LinOSS, LrcSSM) restrict the state-transition matrix to diagonal form, achiev-ing O(log T) temporal parallelism but precluding weighted lateral interac-tion; RSNNs support such interaction but couple it to each temporal step, requiring sequential propagation across T and precluding temporal paral-lelism.PHC’s Multi-Transmission Loop resolves this trade-off by confining cross-neuron interaction to discrete transmission boundaries: within each step k, every neuron’s input is fixed across all T (determined by step k – 1), so the neuron-internal scan and the per-timestep W struct matrix mul-tiplication remain parallelizable. Spatial depth thus accumulates along M transmission steps rather than T, yielding O(M log T) total depth and intro-ducing a new axis of architectural diversity, namely spatial recurrence depth, currently absent from the diagonal SSM family. The fixed M simultaneously acts as implicit regularization against runaway excitation (Rongala et al., 2021; Dehghani et al., 2016), bounding propagation depth regardless of se-quence length, complemented by the SL’s short-term depression; analogous to Deep Equilibrium Models (Bai et al., 2019), this finite-step iteration ap-proximates an infinite recurrent equilibrium, and the Early Exit mechanism (Section 3.6) adaptively truncates when convergence is reached. Notably, PHCSSM achieves competitive accuracy with 1,748–9,485 parameters, one to two orders of magnitude fewer than S5 (67K–206K) and Mamba (67K– 27 401K) (Table 5), suggesting that lateral connections with weight sharing across transmission steps serve as a more parameter-efficient alternative to layer stacking. Because every trainable component, such as structural weights, ALIF time constants, STP parameters, and synaptic delays, satisfies its respec-tive biological constraint by construction throughout training, the resulting model can be directly unrolled as a standard recurrent spiking neural net-work (RSNN) at inference time without post-hoc ANN-to-SNN conversion. While other spiking SSMs (e.g., SPikE-SSM, P-SpikeSSM) can in principle also be executed sequentially, their resulting networks lack the full suite of biological constraints, including Dale’s Law, short-term plasticity, and hier-archical topology, that PHCSSM preserves end-to-end; PHCSSM is therefore the only spiking SSM whose sequential form constitutes a biologically con-strained RSNN. The parallel-to-sequential transition introduces a numerical divergence analogous to the accuracy gap documented in the ANN-to-SNN conversion literature (Deng and Gu, 2021; Bu et al., 2023), but arising from a different mechanism: the multi-transmission loop computes a sequence-level fixed point via Jacobi-style parallel iteration, whereas step-by-step RSNN execution follows a Gauss–Seidel causal trajectory that may converge to a distinct attractor due to the discontinuous spike threshold. Quantitative characterization of this divergence and bridging strategies (e.g., post-training sequential fine-tuning) are deferred to future work. 

Limitations 

Several limitations should be noted. The current evaluation is restricted to binary and multi-class classification at modest state dimensions (D = 16–64) with a two-region hierarchy; scaling behavior under larger configura-tions (4R128, 6R64) remains unknown. The topology mask is hand-designed rather than derived from empirical neuroanatomy. The ablation study (Ta-ble 8) covers three of the six benchmarks, selected to span the range of se-quence lengths and task difficulties (Heartbeat: 405 steps, short; SCP2: 1,152 steps, highest difficulty; MotorImagery: 3,000 steps, long with high channel count). Furthermore, because ALIF and STP are not natively supported on current commercial neuromorphic processors, reliable energy-efficiency esti-mates cannot be provided at this time. 

Future Directions 

The most immediate extension is to validate PHCSSM’s scalability and generality: evaluating larger configurations (4R128, 6R64), regression and generation tasks, and mainstream SSM benchmarks such as the Long Range 28 Arena and Speech Commands would test whether biological constraints con-fer inductive-bias advantages beyond physiological signals. At the architectural level, two directions follow naturally from PHCSSM’s design. First, because the parallel scan can process the full temporal sequence in O(log T), the architecture naturally supports a Liquid State Machine (LSM) operating mode in which the recurrent reservoir weights are frozen and only the readout decoder is trained, substantially reducing the compu-tational cost of reservoir evaluation for long sequences. Second, the hand-crafted topology mask can be replaced with empirical laminar connectivity derived from tract-tracing atlases (e.g., the Allen Mouse Brain Connectivity Atlas) or whole-brain connectomes (e.g., the FlyWire Drosophila connec-tome), advancing PHCSSM toward data-driven functional digital twins of specific neural circuits, where the O(log L) parallel training offers an effi-ciency advantage over traditional sequential SNN simulators at connectome scale. Looking further ahead, the success of Dale’s Law and E/I-balanced con-nectivity as structural regularizers invites exploration of analogous biological priors in domains where SSMs are already established, including language modeling, audio synthesis, and genomics, positioning bio-constrained SSMs as a systematic design methodology rather than a domain-specific curios-ity. The enforced separation of excitatory and inhibitory pathways, together with the fixed projection topology, also provides inherent structural inter-pretability that may be leveraged by mechanistic-interpretability tools to trace information flow through the network. 

6. Conclusion 

This work has presented PHCSSM, a spiking state-space model that integrates five biological constraints: ALIF dynamics, Short-Term Plastic-ity, Dale’s Law, hierarchical connectome topology, and Reward-Modulated STDP, within a fully parallelizable O(log L) framework. By decoupling tem-poral parallel scans from spatial lateral interactions via the Multi-Transmission Loop, PHCSSM achieves competitive accuracy on six UEA physiological benchmarks, including a new state-of-the-art on SCP2 (59.3%), while re-quiring one to two orders of magnitude fewer parameters than comparably performing SSMs. The ablation analysis confirms that each biological con-straint contributes positively and non-redundantly, functioning as stabilizing inductive biases rather than performance bottlenecks. These results estab-29 lish that biologically grounded spatiotemporal recurrence offers a viable and parameter-efficient alternative to conventional layer-stacked SSM architec-tures.
